# Embercrown: The Hold - how to play

*The Hollow took the old kingdom's light. You lead a handful of named survivors to a vale under a mountain, kindle a Hearth, and dig: down through soil and stone, level by level, into caverns no one has seen. Your people grow by what they do, remember what they saw, quarrel, mourn, make masterworks in strange fevers and bury their dead. Nothing resets. There is no win: only the hold, its years, and the stories it leaves in the Annals.*

A colony builder for iPhone Safari (it also plays in any modern browser). This page is for players. The design of record is [EMBERCROWN-COLONY.md](EMBERCROWN-COLONY.md) as amended by [EMBERCROWN-FORTRESS.md](EMBERCROWN-FORTRESS.md).

## Start

- Open the game's address in Safari. For full-screen play, tap **Share > Add to Home Screen**; the game then plays offline.
- **New colony** founds a hold on a fresh map at once: 128 x 128 tiles and 20 levels deep, with the surface 14 levels above the bottom. "Carving the world" shows for a moment while it is made. **Choose the start** lets you pick:
  - **the storyteller**: *The Steady Flame* (a major threat every 5 to 7 days, with long warnings; the default), *The Wandering Ember* (2 to 11 days apart, never two within 2 days) or *The Long Night* (for veterans: harder, more often, shorter warnings);
  - **the difficulty**: *Hearthkeeper* (gentle raids, kind weather), *Warden* (lighter threats), *Steadfast* (the hold as designed) or *Hard* (heavier raids);
  - the hold's name and its map.
- A first-hold guide walks you through the first steps (chop trees, a stockpile, carve into the mountain, a bedroom, then a stair down 2 levels, switching levels and a store by the stairs, a campfire, needs, a grow zone, braziers, and holding by the Hearth on the first night). Fold it away any time.

## Controls (touch)

- Drag to pan, pinch to zoom, tap to select (colonists win the tap). Long-press for the context menu (Draft, Prioritise, Follow, Forbid, Bills).
- The bottom bar opens **Build**, **Orders** (mine, chop, carve a hall, haul, cancel), **Zones** (stockpiles, fields, pens, burial ground) and **Colony** (people, work, schedule, research, stock, military, the Chronicle and the hold).
- In a tool, drag paints it and a long-press stroke erases. **Done** confirms; **Undo** steps back.
- The speed control pauses or runs at 1x, 2x, 3x and 5x. The game pauses itself when you come back to it and when a major letter arrives.
- The portraits along the top are your people: tap one for their card, then **Skills**, **Needs**, **Health**, **Gear** and the rest.

## The hold

- **The Hearth** is the heart of the hold. Keep it fed with fuel; it lights and warms the ground around it and shoots at the Hollow at night.
- **Dig** into the mountain and down into the ground for stone, coal, ore and gems (see *Levels and digging down*). Rock overhead needs support: leave pillars or build props, or a ceiling can fall into the level below.
- **Build** walls, doors, beds, workshops and rooms. Rooms are judged by size, beauty and cleanliness, and a fine room lifts the mood of those who use it.
- **Work** is given by roles and priorities (Colony > Work). Bills on a workshop say what to make and how many.
- **Research** opens new structures and recipes (Colony > Research). The hold grows in rank (Camp, Hamlet, Village and beyond) as its wealth and people grow; each rank changes who comes to the hold.
- **Threats** come on their own: Hollow hordes at night, reavers, thieves, beasts, deep breaches from the rock. A major threat is always warned of ahead: the chip at the top counts down, and a red marker on the map edge shows where it comes from. Draft your people (tap a portrait, then Draft) and tap the ground to hold a line by the Hearth.

## Levels and digging down

The world has depth. You always look at one level at a time; the surface is level 0, and the levels below are -1, -2 and on down. The mountain rises above the surface as real rock you can mine upward.

- **The level capsule** sits at the bottom right in portrait (bottom left in landscape): **up** and **down** arrows around a **level chip** that shows the level ("-3") with the name of its band beside it ("Granite", "Glowcaps"). A badge on an arrow counts your colonists above or below, with a red dot if something hostile is there.
  - Tap an arrow to move one level. Hold it to jump to the next level where something is happening.
  - Drag the chip up or down to scrub through the levels; it pauses at interesting ones.
  - Tap the chip for the **depth ruler**: one row per explored level with its colonists and pending jobs, and **Nearest stairs**. Long-press the chip for **Go to surface** and **Go to deepest dig**.
  - You can only go one level past what you have seen. Dig to see more.
- **Stairs** (Orders > Stairs) join the levels. Tap the ground for a 1 x 1 stair (or drag up to 3 x 3), pick **Down 1, 2, 3, 5 or 10** (or Up), then **Done**. The tool remembers the depth, so a second stair is a tap and Done. A thin bar beside the capsule marks every level the stair reaches; tap a mark to look there.
- **Ramps** (Orders > Ramp) let people walk up and down a slope. **Channel** (Orders > Channel) digs a floor away and opens the level below, leaving a ramp at its foot.
- **Mine** and **Carve hall** have a **Levels** chip: the same room on 1, 2, 3 or 5 levels, down or up.
- **Build** and **Zones** work on the level you are looking at. A building needs a floor; over open air, build a **Built floor** first. One stockpile may span several levels, and haulers carry goods up and down the stairs.
- **What lies below:** soil near the surface, then layers of stone with their own ores, and deeper still the two-level **Glowcap Hollows**, a cavern of glowing fungus with its own creatures. Lower levels are colder in summer and warmer in winter; deep rooms are dark unless you light them.
- **Threats** enter at the surface and come down the stairs. A locked gate holds them for a while, then they break it. Underground, raiders who lose half their number break and run.
- A colonist's portrait shows their level when it is not the one you are on; tapping it takes you there. Alerts and letters take you to the right level too. **Follow** goes up and down the stairs with them.

## Your people grow by what they do

There are no perks to pick. A colonist **learns a perk by doing**: when their skill reaches its level **and** they have done its deed. A miner at Mining 10 who has followed 150 ore seams learns *Seam Follower*; a builder at Construction 10 who has set 20 props or pillars learns *Quick Shoring*; a healer at Medicine 10 who has tended 40 wounds learns *Field Surgeon*. Both perks of a skill can be learned, and a colonist can hold any number.

- When a perk is learned, a line appears ("Bryn has learned Seam Follower: followed 150 ore seams") and it is written in the Chronicle and in the colonist's own log.
- A colonist's **Skills** tab lists the perks they hold and the nearest ones still to come, with what is left: "Next: Seam Follower, mined 112 of 150 ore".
- Colonists keep their skills, passions, traits, friendships, grudges and memories. Inspirations come on their own.

## The Chronicle and the Annals

Everything that matters is written down: arrivals and deaths, deeds the hold has done, perks learned, research, raids repelled, nemeses who swore to come back. Read it in **Colony > Chronicle**, by year. The longer history, with the vale's past and every figure, place and artifact, is the **Annals** (see below); a fallen hold keeps both.

## The life of the hold

### Who they are

Every colonist is a person, not a set of numbers. Their card's **Bio** tab has a **Personality** section: a few plain lines ("Loves a good argument", "Values hard work"), what they like (a stone, a metal, a wood, a food, a creature) and dislike, and their **life goal** ("Make a masterwork", "Raise a family"). A colonist who likes granite is happier in a granite room; one who meets their goal is lifted for the rest of their life. They remember the seasons they lived through, and good and bad days stay with them in the **Needs** tab's thoughts.

### Fevers of craft and artifacts

Now and then a crafter is seized:
- **Ember-touched**: they claim a bench and ask for materials (a card shows what they need). Bring them and they make a **named artifact**, which the Annals remember.
- **Brooding**: after a friend, lover or kin dies, a crafter may ask for a token of the dead (something they owned or made, or a stone from their grave) and makes a memorial in their name.
- **Hollow-whispered**: one who has lived too long in dread takes what they want and makes a grey thing that spreads unease.
A hold of twenty expects a handful of artifacts over its life. Not meeting a demand in time leaves the crafter in a dark mood.

### Offices and nobles

**Colony > Hold > Offices** lists the hold's offices. As the hold grows they open: the **Steward** (keeps stock targets: "keep 40 meals" becomes a bill), the **Reckoner** (exact counts), the **Chief Healer**, the **Keeper of Records** (fuller Annals), the **Marshal** (squad training, uniforms and the alert) and, while the laws are on, the **Warden of Law**. Tap an office to appoint someone. Each office asks for something (an office room, a desk, an armoury rack), shown with a tick or a cross; unmet requests sour the holder after a while.

At 15 colonists the colonists choose a **Thane** (the one they think best of), again at each Year's Turning. At the Hold rank with 20 colonists the Hearthwatch sends a **Lord or Lady of the Hold**. Nobles ask for fine rooms and give **mandates** ("make 3 iron helms in 10 days", "keep the silver"). Met mandates add renown; ignored ones upset the hold and can become a case for the Warden.

### Renown

The hold becomes **known for** what it does: smithing, growing, arms, delving, lore or trade (Colony > Hold shows it). Renown never opens buildings; it steers who visits and who asks to stay.

### Halls, visitors and petitions

A **tavern** (a room with a table and a dice table, stage or tap, with drink in store), a **temple** (a room with a shrine or altar) or a **library** (a study with shelves of books) draws **visitors**: travellers, bards, pilgrims, scholars, smiths and, once the deep is open, deep-traders. They are drawn standing in their hall. A visitor who has stayed a few days and whose skills match what the hold is known for may **petition to stay**: the letter asks, and **Yes** makes them a colonist with their own history in the Annals. Colony > Hold lists the halls and who is in them.

### Families

Two spouses sharing a **Fine** bedroom may have a child (a chance each season, at most one a year for each mother) as long as the hold has food and is under its limit. **Children** do not work or fight; they play and learn a little of what their parents do. **Teens** (12 to 15) work at half speed and never soldier. A colonist's Bio shows their parents and children. **Colony > Hold > Customs > Births** turns births off (on by default).

### Squads and the alert

**Colony > Military** holds the squads. Soldiers join a squad; each squad has a **uniform** (tap a slot to cycle what it asks for), a **training** count for the season (drills at a training yard, with a Marshal), and a **standing order**: **Station** (tap a spot), **Patrol** (tap 2 to 4 points, even on different levels), **Refuge** or **Relieve**. The **alert** sets the whole hold: **Peace** (soldiers work and drill), **Muster** (soldiers drop work, fetch their uniforms from the armoury racks and stand to) and **Shelter** (everyone else goes to the Refuge). "First Watch lacks 2 helms" says what to make.

### Burial and the dead

The dead want rest. Bury a body in a grave, or, when a body was never found (taken by the Hollow, burnt, fallen into the deep), build a **memorial slab** (Build > Civic) and a mason engraves the name. Until then friends and kin feel **Unrested**. **Colony > Hold > The dead** lists who rests and who does not, and where. Every grave and slab is a place in the Annals.

### Crime and justice

With the laws on (the default), a colonist who smashes something in a tantrum, or who breaks a noble's mandate, can be charged. **Colony > Hold > Justice** shows each **case**: the offender, the victim and the witnesses who saw it. The **Warden of Law** (an office that opens at 12 colonists) hears each case and gives a sentence that depends only on how bad it was and on the offender's record: a **labour fine** (extra hours of work) or **confinement** in a **Lockup** (a room with a **cell bed** and a door) for at most 5 days. Repeated serious offences can end in **exile** by letter, which you confirm. There are no beatings and no executions. The laws can be turned off (and on again) once a season: Colony > Hold > Customs > Laws, or the top of the Justice screen. With them off, nothing is a case.

### The Annals

**Colony > Hold > Annals** is the hold's history by **Years**, **Figures**, **Places** and **Artifacts**: founders and newcomers, the dead and how they died, nemeses, births, petitions, offices, artifacts, crimes and sentences, the levels opened, named rooms and tombs. It begins before the hold: the vale's **pre-history** (the old figures, the dormant beasts and the lairs under the mountain), the same for every hold on the same map. Tap a name to follow its links.

### Workshops: add-ons, the winch and research by doing

- **Add-ons** stand beside a bench and add one thing it can do: a **cutting yard** by a mason (ashlar blocks), a **quench trough** by a forge (tempering), a **slag pit** (melting down old gear), an **ember forge** beside an ember pool (no fuel, a better roll, embersteel), and more. A bench's Bills tab lists its add-ons.
- **The winch** (Build > Utility, with Support pillars) stands over an open shaft (dig it with **Channel**): stacks left on its landing ride down to the shaft's foot, and stacks at the foot ride up, three at a time. Haulers use it on their own.
- **Research by doing**: about a third of the techs can also be **discovered** by working a material, reading a lore tablet from a vault, or hosting a visiting scholar. Hold a tech in the Research tab to see how.

### Every threat has a reason

No threat comes for nothing. Each one names its cause in its warning letter and in the Annals: a grievance (a faction you wronged, a nemesis who swore to return), a rumour of the hold's wealth, the Hollow's hunger, a hard winter, a seep the hold woke. A hold that gives no cause sees fewer threats.

### Life underground

Cave loam grows **glowcaps**, **greyshank** (fungus timber), **mere-reed** and lichen with no sunlight; a grow zone on cave loam farms them. The caverns have their own creatures, who keep to their own levels. A wet **seep** in a wall can be lined to dry it.

## A hold can fall, and the fall stands

There is no win and no game over screen to retry from. A colony goes on as long as someone lives to tend the Hearth.

- If no colonist is **free and able** for 24 game hours (everyone dead, downed with no one to carry them to a bed, or lost to a break), the hold **falls**. *The Hearth goes cold* shows how long it lasted, its deeds, the dead and how they died, and the last pages of its story.
- A fallen hold is a **Ruin**. Its card on the title screen is marked **Fallen**, with **Annals**, **View the ruin** (the map as it was, to look at but not change), **Export** (the save and the Annals as text) and **Delete**. There is no Continue.
- A new colony is always a new map. Nothing carries over.

## Saving, backups and devices

- The game saves on its own (every minute, at dawn and whenever you leave), into two rotating copies and a dawn backup, all in the browser. **Menu > Save now** saves at once; **Menu > Back up** writes a file you can keep.
- **Import a backup** on the title screen loads such a file (from this version or an earlier one). Up to 8 colonies can be kept. Each colony card shows how deep it has dug ("Deepest: -9").
- **Settings > Storage** shows what is stored. **Recover from a device problem** lists a colony's stored copies with their dates, for when the phone or the browser lost your progress. It is not a way to undo a fall: a fallen hold stays fallen in its Annals, and a copy loaded from before a fall says so in the Chronicle.
- Safari may clear a website's storage after 7 days without a visit. The Home Screen app keeps its own storage; **Menu > Move colony to the app** carries a colony from Safari into it.
- Players of the old roguelite: its saves are not part of this game. **Settings > Storage > Old roguelite saves** exports them to a file and then removes them (this row stays for one release).

## Settings

The hold's own customs are set in the game, not here: **Colony > Hold > Customs** turns **Births** and the **Laws** off or on (the laws once a season).

Text size (100%, 115% or 130%), reduce motion, colour-blind safe overlays, pauses (when a raid is near, on letters, when someone is down), an advanced work grid, two-finger tap to pause, battery saver, a left-handed layout, the back-up prompt, sound, music and volume.

## What changed in this release (water and quiet, Z4a)

- **Hand pump.** Research Waterworks, build a pump over water and face it the way to fill. Haulers work it, a
  water wheel within 6 tiles on its level drives it, and a linked lever set to Locked stops it.
- **Floodgates can be built** (Mechanisms research). **Windows** (Glassblowing) keep out feet and water, let light
  through, bound a room like a wall and add beauty to the rooms beside them.
- **Wells by level.** A well below the surface needs water in or under it; a watered well doubles firefighting
  within 15 tiles on its level.
- **Section view.** From the depth ruler or the level chip's menu: a side cut through every level you have seen.
  Touch a level to go there.
- **Sound of the hold.** Each depth has its own ambience that changes with the season; Battery saver turns it off.
- **A "No bed for the sick" alert** when a tainted or infected colonist lies on the ground, and higher-contrast faint text.

## The release before (the deep fights back)

- **Caverns are a door you open.** Nothing comes up a deep passage until your miners break into its cavern. Once
  open, cavern packs and the Lamplost (who may trade, or steal) can climb to you. A threat chip always names the
  level and the reason: Opened cavern, Near lair or Woken seep, and every deep threat is warned at least 6 hours ahead.
- **Great beasts.** Each hold has its own beasts, born from its seed, plus three legends. You cannot call one; each
  comes only for a reason the hold gave it (a cavern opened near its lair, the Ember Seal mined). A hurt beast flees,
  heals with the season and may return. Slaying one leaves trophies and a line in the Annals.
- **Fighting on levels.** Archers shoot down through open shafts and channels up to two levels, never through rock.
  Murder holes and fortification walls give cover; flyers come down shafts; sappers dig down toward you.
- **Water and ember flow.** Mine under the river and the level below floods. Floodgates, doors and raising bridges
  stop fluid; link a lever to them from any level (the lever-link sheet lists each link with its level). Flood and
  ember alerts name their level: one tap takes the view there.

## Earlier (the life of the hold)

- Colonists have personalities, likes, life goals and memories (Bio tab), and fevers of craft make named artifacts and memorials.
- Offices, the Thane and the Lord, mandates and noble requests (Colony > Hold > Offices).
- The Annals of years, figures, places and artifacts, with the vale's pre-history (Colony > Hold > Annals).
- Burial, memorial slabs and the Unrested (Colony > Hold > The dead).
- Squads, uniforms, training and the alert (Colony > Military).
- Families and children (births on by default; Colony > Hold > Customs).
- Taverns, temples and libraries with visitors who may petition to stay.
- Crime and justice with the Warden of Law and the Lockup (on by default; can be turned off once a season).
- Renown, bench add-ons, the winch, research by doing, cavern farming, and a reason for every threat.
- Buildings are paced by materials, research and skill, not by the hold's rank.
- Colonies from the previous version load and continue: the new records start empty and fill as the hold lives on.

## Earlier (depth)

- The world has levels: a new colony is 20 levels deep, with stairs, ramps, channels and built floors, and the Glowcap Hollows below. See *Levels and digging down*.
- **Colonies from the previous version gain their depth when you continue them.** Your map becomes the surface level; the old mountain stands as real rock above it (tunnels keep their ceilings), and new levels lie below. One card says so the first time ("Your hold now has depth"), and the Chronicle records the day the hold learned the depth of the world. Everything on the map stays where it was.
- A colony is not tick-for-tick the same after the upgrade, because the rules for paths, warmth and raids now work in depth. From then on it saves and loads exactly as before. Until its first save in the new format succeeds, the previous copy is kept, so a failed upgrade loses nothing.

## Earlier (Stage 2.9)

- The old classic roguelite and its link are gone.
- Perks are earned by deeds; nothing is ever picked.
- Ironhold is gone. The hardest difficulty is **Hard**, with the same backups as every colony. An Ironhold colony from an earlier version loads as Hard.
- There is no Challenge Pyre and no Lord to summon, and no victory: great beasts and long arcs replace boss goals. Wonders are now prestige buildings.
- A fallen hold stays fallen and can be read as a Ruin.
- Colonies saved by the previous version load and carry on. A colonist who was waiting on a perk choice settles into one of the pair, and the Chronicle says so ("Bryn settled into the ways of a Seam Follower").
