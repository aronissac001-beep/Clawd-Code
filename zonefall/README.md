# Zonefall 4

A first-person, open-world survival base-builder for the browser that you can install on an iPhone Home Screen. Wash up on a beach with a rock and a torch, gather, build a real base out of foundations and walls, raid monuments for loot, and defend your Tool Cupboard from raids every night.

Zonefall 4 is a full first-person remake with a bright, cel-shaded comic-book look. Everything has hard-banded toon lighting and black ink outlines, with hand-drawn hatching in the textures.

**Play:** https://aronissac001-beep.github.io/Clawd-Code/zonefall/ — open it in Safari, then Share → Add to Home Screen, and play in landscape.

## What's in it
- **First person:**
  - Shooting is real 3D ballistics: bullets drop and travel, headshots crit, and cover blocks shots.
  - Recoil and bloom, aim-down-sights with scopes, sprinting, jumping and crouching. Crouching also keeps you hidden.
  - You chop, mine and build by looking at things.
- **Comic style:**
  - Ink outlines and toon lighting.
  - Floating damage numbers, with crits that shout.
  - Loot beams in rarity colours over dropped weapons and rare loot.
  - Eye-level rain and snow, and readable blue nights.
- **One open island** (1.5 km across), generated from a seed: beaches, grassland, forests, a snowy north, an arid south, mountains, lakes and a ring road.
  - **Rivers:** four of them run from springs in the hills down to the sea, each in its own valley. The valleys get deeper where the land is higher. Roads and the railway cross them on bridges.
  - **Dry ravines:** cut into the hill country.
  - **Power lines:** strung across the island on steel pylons, from the Reactor Sector out to the coast and between the big sites.
- **Ziplines:**
  - Stand at any pylon and tap **Zipline**. The button shows which way the line goes (downhill, uphill or level) and how long it is. You face the pylon you want to ride to.
  - You climb up the pylon, hook a pulley onto the cable and slide to the next pylon and on down the line. You pick up speed downhill.
  - While you ride, the button changes to **Let go** and shows how far you'd fall. Jump or press E also lets go. If you let go over deep water, you drop in and swim (water breaks the fall).
  - At the end of the line you slow down and slide down the last pylon, so there's no fall damage.
  - Up there, nothing on the ground can reach you.
- **Play with friends (co-op):**
  - Up to 4 players, phone-to-phone, with no game server. One player taps **Play with friends → Host a game** and reads out the 5-letter room code. Friends tap **Join** and type it.
  - Everyone plays on the host's island, starts next to them and shares the day/night clock. You see each other as your own custom characters with name tags, plus what you're holding and your vehicles.
  - Shots and melee are replicated. The host can switch PvP (friendly fire) on or off; it's off by default.
  - Deaths show in the kill feed, and there's chat with quick phrases.
  - **One shared world.** The host's phone runs the island and everyone sees the same thing:
    - The same enemies, wildlife and bots. Guests can fight them, and shots and explosions land on the host's copy.
    - The same loot. Crates, barrels, drops, corpses and storage boxes are shared, so if one player empties a crate it's empty for everyone.
    - The same bases. Walls, foundations, upgrades, doors (open or shut), deployables and damage are shared, so you can build one base together.
    - The same gathering. Felled trees, mined rocks and picked plants are gone for everyone.
    - The same train timetable and the same airdrops (a guest's supply signal calls the plane for everyone).
    - The same vehicles. Every buggy, minicopter, rowboat and motorboat is in the same place for everyone. When a friend drives one, you see that exact vehicle move, and it stays where they park it. Damage, fuel, wrecks and respawns are shared too.
    - One driver per vehicle: you can't get into one a friend is using. If two of you jump in at the same moment, the first one keeps it.
  - A guest's co-op progress isn't saved, so their solo run stays safe.
  - **If joining fails:**
    - Both phones need internet and the same version. If one is out of date, the join screen says so; close Zonefall completely and reopen it.
    - Keep the host's game open on screen while friends join. If the host switches apps, the room reconnects by itself when they come back (the co-op screen shows "Room open").
    - Phones on different networks (for example mobile data and someone else's Wi-Fi) connect through a free relay server.
- **Profile and character creator:** pick a name, skin, hair style and colour, beard, shirt, trousers or shorts, a hat and an accent colour, with a live 3D preview. It dresses your first-person arms and how friends see you.
- **Vehicles:**
  - **Dune buggies** sit on the roadsides. Use the stick or WASD to drive, and pull back to brake and reverse. They're fast on roads, slower off-road, and run people over.
  - **Motorcycles with sidecars** are parked on the roadsides too. They ride like the buggy (fast on roads, slower off-road), lean into the turns, and carry a pillion passenger and one in the sidecar.
  - **Minicopters** wait at the airfield, by Haven Market and out in the fields. The nose follows where you look. The stick flies you forward or sideways, ▲/Space climbs, ▼/C descends, and it hovers on its own.
  - All of these burn low-grade fuel, which is topped up from your pockets when you climb in.
  - They take damage from crashes, falls and explosions, and wrecked ones respawn later.
  - **Horses** stand saddled at hitching rails by Haven Market, Saltmarsh Village and Hollow Creek, and a few graze out in the grassland. They need no fuel.
    - The stick or WASD walks and trots. Push the stick all the way forward (or hold Shift) to gallop. Galloping uses up the horse's stamina (shown on the HUD), and it gets it back when you trot, walk or stop. Pull back to stop and back up.
    - Horses slow down on steep slopes, wade through shallow water but won't swim, and step over stumps and other low clutter.
    - A horse carries you and one passenger behind the saddle. It gets hurt by crashes, falls and explosions; a killed horse collapses (it doesn't explode), throws you off, and a new one turns up at its spot later.
  - Flying the minicopter keeps you out of reach of ground enemies and animals.
  - Jumping out high up or at speed hurts, and an abandoned heli falls out of the sky.
  - **Passengers:** every vehicle carries a driver and two passengers (a horse carries one). In co-op, walk up to a friend's vehicle and tap **Ride as passenger**. The buggy has a front seat and a jump seat on the engine deck, the motorcycle has a pillion seat and the sidecar, the minicopter has a bench on each side, and the boats have bow and stern seats.
    - Passengers can look around, aim and shoot. Jump or E gets you out (out of a boat in deep water, you go over the side and swim).
    - If the driver gets out, you can take over: **Take the wheel**, **Take the reins** or **Take the handlebars**.
- **Swimming:** wade out past your depth and you swim, head and shoulders above the water. Swim where you look (sprint for a faster crawl). Jump rises and crouch dives.
  - Under water the view turns blue and sound goes muffled. An air bar shows how long you can hold your breath; when it runs out you start to drown.
  - You can't use weapons while you swim, and nothing on land can follow you out. Falling into deep water (off a cliff, a bridge or a zipline) doesn't hurt.
- **The cargo ship:**
  - From day 2 a freighter appears offshore on most days and sails round the island, calling at each harbor in turn. At each stop it ties up for about five minutes with a fresh crew and fresh crates, then sails on, and after the last stop it heads back out to sea.
  - Climb the gangway from the quay, or pick off the crew at the rail from the quay below (the pipe railing doesn't stop bullets, the hull does). The deck has container stacks and military crates, the crew quarters have lockers, and up the ramp the bridge has an elite crate.
  - Its armed crew guard it.
  - A **locked cargo crate** sits on deck. Hacking it takes two minutes, and more crew come up from below while it runs.
  - The horn warns you a minute before it casts off. Anyone still aboard gets put ashore, and the ship sails back out to sea.
  - It shows on the map and compass while it's in.
- **Boats:**
  - **Rowboats:** slow, need no fuel, turn on the spot. You find them pulled up on beaches, at the fishing village and on lakes and rivers.
  - **Motorboats:** fast inflatables with an outboard engine that burns low-grade fuel. Harbors, lighthouses, the fishing village and beaches have them.
  - Both bob on the swell, lean into turns, run aground in the shallows, and can be wrecked and sunk (they respawn later).
  - Get out onto a shore, a jetty or the docked cargo ship.
- **The railway:**
  - A track loops through the interior with four halts. Each halt has a platform, a shelter and loot.
  - A little freight train runs the loop on a timetable. It sounds its horn, dwells at each halt and then pulls away.
  - Climb aboard a flatcar (E), walk about, shoot from it, and step off at a platform or jump off anywhere.
  - The flatcars carry cargo crates that restock as the train runs.
  - Standing on the track while the train is moving hurts.
- **Monuments:**
  - No two sites are quite alike. About half are built mirrored, and each one is weathered with oil stains, cracks, puddles, litter, rubble and weeds. Each has odds and ends round its edge (tyres, pallets, drums, wrecks, hay, sandbags) and telegraph poles with sagging wires.
  - Most sites also get one extra spot with a bit of loot: a crashed wreck, a stack of shipping containers, a lookout tower, a squatter's camp or a lock-up shed.
  - Gas stations come as the classic forecourt, a truck stop with a diner, or burnt out with the canopy fallen in.
  - Supermarkets can have a loading dock out back, or an overgrown car park with a car nosed through the front window.
  - Scav camps are tents, scrap-metal shacks, or a convoy camp walled in by an old bus and a truck.
  - Village houses are repainted, burnt-out shells or overgrown with ivy, house by house.
  - Haven Market: a safe zone with vendors, guards and recyclers.
    - It also has the **Lucky Duck Casino**, where you gamble scrap at blackjack against the dealer or on rubber-duck races.
    - Blackjack: a 6-deck shoe, 3:2 for a natural, and you can double or split.
    - Duck races: odds change every race, and the ducks on the real track in the room race along with the screen.
  - **Saltmarsh Village:** a peaceful fishing village and a second safe zone. The Bait & Tackle shop sells rods and bait and pays full value for your catch.
  - Smaller sites: gas stations, supermarkets, lighthouses, Hollow Creek villages and quarries. These are unguarded and full of loot. Scav camps still have scavs.
  - **Radio Outposts:** fenced hilltop relays full of loot. Some have a three-deck steel tower wrapped round the radio mast, with a cable bridge into a two-storey comms house. Others have a watch cabin on stilts and a shack with a roof terrace. The best loot is at the top.
  - **Lighthouses:** climb the tower. A spiral stair winds round a stone core up to the watch room, and a gallery runs all the way round outside. Some have a keeper's house and a boathouse. Others have a coaster run aground on the beach: get in through the torn hull, or go up the gangway to the deck and wheelhouse.
  - **Stone Quarries:** a terraced rock face with haul ramps up to two benches. The explosives store sits on the top bench, and an old timbered tunnel leads to a chamber with ore. Some quarries have a crusher house and stacked site cabins. Others have an ore-bin trestle fed by a mine railway, and a headframe over a shaft.
  - **Caves:** rocky outcrops with winding tunnels, an old camp, crystal grottoes full of ore and something living in the deepest chamber. **Deep caves** hide a military stash and lurkers.
  - **Stone arches** and **hoodoo spires:** landmarks with a little loot tucked away.
  - **Climb up:** every watchtower has stairs to its platform, at Haven Market, the Scav Camp, the Dish Array, the Airfield and Garrison 9. Supermarkets, gas stations and the Train Yard control tower have stairs to rooftops with loot, and the Harbor warehouse has a mezzanine with crates.
  - **Apartment Blocks:** ruined three-storey blocks you can climb. An outside stair leads to the first floor, inside stairs go up to the second floor and the roof, and there's loot and enemies on every level, with a sniper nest up top.
  - Larger sites: the Dish Array, Harbor, Train Yard, Water Treatment and the bandit-held **Observatory**, whose green vault is powered from its generator shed. These have keycard vaults, and some need a fuse or a lever.
    - **Dish Array:** the green vault is on the ground floor of a two-storey control block with a roof deck, and a skywalk runs out from its upper floor. Some arrays have three dishes on bunkers joined by catwalks. Others have one giant dish on a receiver hall whose roof you can reach.
    - **Water Treatment:** bridges run out over the clarifiers, and a catwalk crosses the hot aeration basins to the blower deck. A sniper holds the filter tower. The pump house (fuse box) and the admin block (blue vault) both have upper floors and roof decks. Some works have a long reservoir with a sluice house on its bridge.
  - Military sites: the Airfield and Garrison 9, where the Warlord boss lives.
  - The Reactor Sector, with radiation, anomalies, artifacts and lurkers.
  - Outlaw Forts, which you raid with satchels or C4.
- **Rust-style building:**
  - 3 m foundations, with walls, doorways, windows, low walls and roofs on their edges.
  - **Up to three storeys:** a roof is the floor of the storey above. Walls go on the edges of any floor, and you aim at the floor you want to build on.
  - **Stairs** fill one cell and climb one storey. Face the way you want to walk up (R turns them) and leave the floor above them open.
  - **Ladders** lean on a wall or a floor edge. Walk into one (or tap Climb) and push forward to go up or back to go down. Jump lets go.
  - **Triangles:** triangle foundations snap onto any free edge of a square or triangle foundation. Triangle floors go over them, or hang off the edge of the floor you stand on. Walls, doorways, windows and low walls fit all three edges, including the slanted ones.
  - Five tiers: twig, wood, stone, sheet metal and armored. The hammer upgrades, repairs, rotates and removes pieces.
  - High external walls and gates.
  - The build bar lists each cost resource on its own line, so it fits on small phone screens.
- **Fishing:**
  - Craft a fishing rod (80 wood, 12 cloth), or buy one at Saltmarsh Village.
  - Tap FIRE to cast where you're looking. When the bobber dips, tap to strike, then hold FIRE to reel in. Ease off when the tension bar goes red, or the line snaps.
  - Lakes give perch and trout. The sea gives mackerel and tuna. Salmon turn up in both, and very rarely a golden fish.
  - Bait (made from raw meat, berries or mushrooms) brings bites faster and bigger fish.
  - Cook fish on a campfire, or sell them.
- **New guns:**
  - Ratchet Crossbow
  - Nailgun (crafted nails)
  - Ridgeback semi-auto carbine
  - Longreach bolt-action sniper with a high-power scope
  - Thresher 60-round light machine gun
  - **Waterpipe Shotgun:** a single-shell pipe gun you can make without a workbench.
  - **Semi-Auto Pistol:** a 10-round sidearm (Workbench 1, blueprint).
  - **Magnum Revolver:** six heavy rounds and a hard kick (Workbench 2, blueprint).
  - **Tactical SMG:** fast, accurate, 30 pistol rounds (Workbench 3, blueprint).
  - **Lancer Rifle:** an automatic rifle with less damage than the Assault Rifle but far less kick (Workbench 3, blueprint).
  - **Combat Auto-Shotgun:** 7 shells, one blast per tap (Workbench 3, blueprint).

  You craft them at the workbench (blueprints for the bigger ones) or find them in crates, airdrops and the cargo ship. Scavs, bandits, outlaws, troopers, hazmat sentries, brutes and the cargo crew carry them too.
- **Weapon attachments:** tap a gun in your inventory to see its slots (sight, muzzle, rail, magazine), then tap or drag an attachment in. Tap a fitted one to take it off. Each gun takes a sensible set: pistols can take a holosight or silencer, shotguns a holosight or brake, rifles everything.
  - **Holosight:** a red-dot sight with a little zoom and tighter aimed fire.
  - **4x and 8x scopes:** real zoom when aiming, with a scope overlay and slower look. They are clumsier from the hip.
  - **Silencer:** enemies only hear your shots from about a fifth as far, and there is no muzzle flash. Range drops slightly.
  - **Muzzle brake:** about half the recoil, but the shots scatter more.
  - **Laser sight:** a red dot where you point and much tighter hip fire.
  - **Weapon flashlight:** a brighter, longer beam than the built-in gun light, on from dusk. It follows the gun flashlight setting.
  - **Extended magazine:** 40% more rounds, a little slower to reload. Taking it off returns the extra rounds.
  - Attachments show on the gun in your hands and in enemies' hands. They stay on the gun when you move, store, drop or save it. You can find them in crates (most in military, elite and airdrop crates), craft them from Workbench 1 to 3 (most need a blueprint), or research them. Some enemies carry guns with attachments, and the guns they drop keep them.
- **Loot that resets:** crates vanish once looted (straight away if empty, a couple of minutes after opening if not) and come back later with fresh loot.
- **Smarter enemies:**
  - They find their way round walls and buildings instead of running into them. When the straight line is blocked they plan a short path with a local grid search, and they take stairs and ladders to reach you on another floor. Floors between storeys block their view.
  - They call out your position to their friends, flank you as a group and push in while you reload.
  - They reload when you're out of sight, bandage themselves up behind cover, and run off to regroup when badly hurt.
- **Inventory quality of life:**
  - **Clearer layout:** equipment on the left (head, body, bag and two artifact slots, with outline icons when empty), then the belt (highlighted, numbered, with the slot in your hand marked) and the backpack, then the open box and the item details. On a phone each column scrolls on its own.
  - **Item details:** rarity, loaded rounds, stats with bars, and markers comparing them with the gun or tool in your hand or the armour you wear. Attachments list what they change and which guns they fit. The actions are Use, Equip, Learn, Fit, Unload, Strip attachments, Split, Move and Drop.
  - **Drag and drop shows where things can go:** while you drag, every valid slot is outlined (an attachment's guns in yellow) and everything else dims. Drop armour, helmets, bags and artifacts straight onto their equipment slots.
  - Slots are tinted by rarity, and guns show a pip for each attachment.
  - Smashed barrels drop their loot straight into your pockets, with a +item popup for each. Anything that doesn't fit drops on the ground, and you can switch this off in Settings.
  - **Sort** buttons for your backpack and for any box. They merge stacks and order items by type.
  - **Deposit all** and **Stack matching** (only what the box already holds), next to Take all.
  - Double-tap an item to send it to the other side: into the open box, or between belt and backpack.
  - Press and hold an item to drag it to any slot. On desktop, just drag.
  - **Split ½** and **Take 1** for stacks, or a slider to split off any amount.
- **Real doors:** walking into your own door swings it open, and it closes behind you. Raiders and animals have to break doors.
- **Tool Cupboard:** it claims the land, stores resources and feeds your turrets. From night 4 raiders come for it every few nights, each raid bigger than the last, and if it falls the run ends.
  - **Upkeep:** every in-game day the cupboard takes a share of what your base cost to build: wood for wood and twig, stone for stone, metal fragments for sheet metal, HQM for armored, plus doors and high walls. The share grows with the base: 6% for the first 12 pieces, 10% up to 40, 15% up to 100 and 22% beyond. A hut costs a handful of wood. A 2-storey stone house of about 30 pieces costs about 180 stone a day.
  - The cupboard window shows the upkeep per day and how long your stock lasts.
  - If the cupboard runs dry, or a piece is outside any claim, the piece decays. Twig goes in about a minute, wood in a day and armored in about two days.
- **Survival:** health, food, water, bleeding and radiation. You hunt, cook, smelt, craft with workbench tiers, research blueprints and recycle.
- **Human-like enemies:**
  - They react a beat after spotting you, and their aim settles the longer they track you.
  - They shoot worse on the move, and getting hit spoils their aim.
  - In a fight they reposition, then plant and fire in bursts.
  - They duck into cover to reload or when badly hurt.
  - When they lose you, they search your last position and sweep the area before returning to post. Idle guards glance around and stroll.
- **AI survivors:** bots that play like other players. They gather, craft, build their own bases, loot, hunt, fight, hold grudges and chat. Some are friendly and can join your team; some shoot on sight. They pause to look around between jobs, face you when they talk, and are a little less accurate than before.
  - **They progress over the days.** They make better tools, guns, armour and ammo as they find blueprints and build workbenches. They loot crates, roadside barrels and, once better armed, the tougher monuments. They take spare components and valuables to a recycler and smelt ore in their furnace.
  - **Their bases grow:** a small house, then a second storey up the stairs, then stone, then an annex with a big box, then sheet metal. They rebuild lost pieces, keep their cupboard stocked for upkeep and store loot in their boxes.
  - **Raids are rare, and you get a warning.** A hostile survivor only comes for your base once it has stockpiled enough satchels to blow the cheapest way in to your cupboard. You hear a rumour while it stocks up, and it announces the raid at dusk. There's at most one survivor raid every three days, and none before day 5.
  - **Their stuff is lootable:** get inside a survivor's base and you can open its boxes and cupboard. They won't like it.
- **Events:**
  - Airdrops, supply signals, and a patrol gunship you can shoot down.
  - rare night raids that grow with each one, and boss raiders.
- **Loot everywhere:** loot tables now include tools, the bow, boomstick, armour, bags, satchels and every blueprint. Containers restock faster, and there are more stashes.
- **Roadside loot** comes in small clusters every so often along the roads, like in Rust: three things to loot, always including a crate, sometimes beside a wrecked car, with empty stretches in between.
- **Meta progression:** perks persist between runs.

## Controls
- **Touch:**
  - Drag on the left side to move. Push the stick all the way forward to sprint.
  - Drag anywhere on the right side to look.
  - The fire buttons also steer while you hold them. There is a big one bottom-right and a small one on the left edge.
  - The other buttons are aim (eye), jump, crouch, reload and heal.
  - The orange hand button is the context action. Hold it to gather.
  - Tap a belt slot to switch items.
- **Keyboard and mouse:** click the game to capture the mouse.

  | Key | Action |
  |---|---|
  | WASD | Move |
  | Shift | Sprint |
  | Mouse | Look |
  | Left click | Fire or swing |
  | Right click or V | Aim |
  | Space | Jump |
  | C or Ctrl | Crouch |
  | E | Use or gather |
  | R | Reload |
  | H | Heal |
  | 1–6 or wheel | Belt slots |
  | B | Build |
  | X | Hammer |
  | Tab | Inventory |
  | Q | Craft (rotates the piece while building) |
  | M | Map |
  | Esc | Pause |

- **Buttons:** every button, menu, build-bar item and inventory slot reacts the instant your finger lifts, even while you hold the move stick.
- **Settings:** look sensitivity, field of view, invert Y, aim assist and graphics quality. There is also a Repair button that clears cached files.

## Tech
Plain static files with no build step. It uses ES modules and [Three.js r170](https://threejs.org/), vendored in `lib/`. Co-op uses [PeerJS](https://peerjs.com/) (MIT, vendored in `lib/`). Its free public server only introduces the phones; game traffic then flows directly between them over WebRTC.
- **Models and icons:** every model is procedural low-poly geometry built in code (`src/gfx/models_*.js`). The item icons are pixel art drawn in code (`js/art_icons.js`).
- **Comic look:** two pieces make it:
  - A toon patch on the shared Lambert shader chunk (`src/gfx/materials.js`).
  - A post pass (`src/gfx/ink.js`) that inks silhouettes and creases. It finds them from the Laplacian of 1/z in the depth buffer, then grades colour and tone-maps.
- **Rendering cost:** static monuments merge into one mesh per texture, with material colours folded into vertex colours. Vegetation is instanced in 64–128 m blocks. Crates, nodes and drops live in a distance-culled grid (`src/gfx/cullgrid.js`). The far plane follows the fog.
- **Audio and offline:** the audio is synthesized with WebAudio and is positional, panned by where you look.
  - **Music** is synthesized live and follows what you're doing. It has a main theme on the menu, warm guitar and marimba when you explore by day, a music box at night, a tense pulse near the big monuments, a lounge groove in safe zones, and drums, bass and brass in a fight (heavier during raids). Moods crossfade, and the exploring music takes breaks. Its volume is under **Settings → Music**.
  - **Ambience:** wind gets stronger up high and in storms. There's rain, surf near the sea, crickets and the odd owl at night, birds by day, and dripping and a low hum in caves.
  - **Sound effects:** guns have a crack, a body, a thump up close and an echo off the land at range. Explosions rumble and rain debris. Footsteps sound different on grass, sand, snow, wood, metal, stone and water. Hits give a hit-marker tick, and a heartbeat warns you when you're nearly dead.
 A network-first service worker lets the game run offline from the Home Screen.
